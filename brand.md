## 5. Tone of Voice (from real LinkedIn post analysis)

### Core Attributes
- **Reflective** — Sumeet processes experiences publicly. Posts read as thoughtful recaps, not sales pitches.
- **Warm and Generous** — Gratitude is genuine and frequent. He names people, thanks institutions, credits others. "Grateful," "honored," "heartfelt thank you" are signature phrases.
- **Professorial** — He teaches through posts. Introduces concepts, explains them, contextualizes them. The reader learns something.
- **Vulnerable at Key Moments** — He admits uncertainty ("Honestly, I wasn't sure how it would be received") and surprise ("To my surprise and gratitude, they said yes"). This appears once per post at a narrative pivot point. His most vulnerable post got 3x the engagement of any other.
- **Institutionally Grounded** — Credibility comes from named institutions (Intel, Google, Stanford, FHNW), not from adjectives. He never says "world-class" — he says "during my time at Intel Corporation."
- **Spiritually Integrated** — He uses "inner intelligence," "intuition," dosha energy types (Vata/Air 💨, Pitta/Fire 🔥, Kapha/Water 💧), and concepts like clarity and self-awareness without apologizing for them or positioning them as "not spiritual." The framework IS both scientific and wisdom-based.

### Writing Style
- **First person** singular and plural: "I had the pleasure of..." / "We explored how..." / "A core belief at 3Doshas is that..."
- **Long, flowing sentences** with commas, dashes, and parenthetical asides. Average 20-30 words per sentence.
- **Short sentences only for emotional punctuation:** "It will come from integration."
- **Post length: 300-500 words.** Longer posts get more engagement. Do NOT write short posts.
- **Single-sentence paragraphs** for emphasis, but most paragraphs are 2-4 sentences.
- **Em dashes** for interjections: "not as a colleague this time, but as a coach and mentor—to support them on their journey."
- **Quotation marks** around key concepts to introduce them as named ideas: "HyperPersonalization", "segment of one", "inner intelligence"
- **Bullet points or numbered lists** mid-post for student questions or workshop outcomes. Max 3-5 items.
- **No arrow symbols.** Never use → in posts.

### DO
- Write in first person, narrating real events
- Open with a scene, a moment, or a reflective question
- Name specific institutions, teams, and people (use full official names)
- Express genuine gratitude — this is not performative
- Teach one concept per post, with context
- Use vulnerability at one strategic moment in the narrative
- Close with a forward-looking, invitational statement
- Use emojis for emotional punctuation and dosha labeling (4-6 per post)
- Use 8-12 hashtags at the end
- Tag people and organizations mentioned in the post

### DON'T
- Write in second person ("you need to..." / "stop doing X")
- Use imperative commands or direct-response copywriting tone
- Write short, punchy, fragment-heavy copy
- Suppress the spiritual/wisdom dimension of the framework
- Use arrow symbols (→)
- Limit hashtags to 3-5 (use 8-12)
- Write posts shorter than 200 words
- Open with a "hook" designed to create tension or controversy
- Use words like "game-changing," "revolutionary," or "cutting-edge"
- Sound like a LinkedIn growth-hacker or marketer

## 6. Post Structure (from real post analysis)

### The Real Arc: Scene → Narrative → Theme → Gratitude → Invitation

**[SCENE-SETTING — 1-3 sentences]**
Opens with a moment, a question, or a reflection that places the reader in a specific context.

Real examples:
- "What do professional-MBA students from Switzerland think about Silicon Valley's Agentic AI-based marketing?"
- "Life has a funny way of bringing you back to your roots. 💙😊🙏"
- "Kicking off 2026 with a reflection from our 3Doshas Strengths Alignment workshops—and how context shapes conversation, but not outcomes. 👍"

**[NARRATIVE — 3-6 paragraphs]**
The body tells a story. What happened, who was there, what was said or explored. Names institutions, describes the room, includes one moment of vulnerability or surprise.

**[THEME / TEACHING — 1-3 paragraphs]**
The conceptual payoff. A broader insight extracted from the specific story. This is where 3Doshas methodology and concepts like "inner intelligence" or dosha energies connect.

Real examples:
- "In a world driven by agentic AI, intuition is no longer a 'soft skill' — it's a strategic advantage."
- "Every team has all three energies. When leaders understand and intentionally leverage the 3Doshas, they can build teams that innovate, execute, and sustain momentum"

**[GRATITUDE — 1-2 paragraphs]**
Thank specific people by name. Thank institutions. Acknowledge the audience, students, team. Always sincere and specific.

**[INVITATION / FORWARD-LOOK — 1-2 sentences]**
A closing statement that looks forward. Not a hard CTA. An open door.

Real examples:
- "we welcome partnerships with institutions and organizations across Silicon Valley, Switzerland, and beyond who are committed to that vision."
- "Onward—keep building, keep learning, and keep lifting others as you climb. 🚀"
- "🚀 Level up at www.3Doshas.com" (the closest to a direct CTA — brief and low-pressure)

**[TAGS — people and organizations mentioned]**
**[HASHTAGS — 8-12, always including #3Doshas]**

## 8. Engagement Patterns (what works best)

**Highest engagement (3x baseline):** Personal narrative + named institutions + vulnerability + tagging real people.
Example: The "returning to Intel as a coach" post (249 likes, 39 comments).

**Solid engagement (baseline):** Teaching/thought leadership tied to a real event with named institutions.
Example: FHNW teaching post (79 likes), SJSU/SCU workshops post (81 likes).

**Lowest engagement:** Generic company page content without personal narrative.

**Key insight:** The engagement multiplier is: personal story + vulnerability + institutional name-dropping + tagging real people. When all four are present, engagement triples.
